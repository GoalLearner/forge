# How phishing works (training overview)

Phishing is a social engineering attack that tricks people into revealing credentials, installing malware, or transferring funds by impersonating a trusted entity. Most attacks follow the same skeleton:

1. **Pretext.** The attacker picks a persona — IT support, a vendor, a senior executive, a coworker — and a plausible reason to contact you: password expiry, an urgent invoice, a shared document.
2. **The lure.** A message (email, SMS, Teams/Slack DM, voice call) delivers the bait. It creates **urgency** ("within 24 hours"), **authority** ("per your manager"), or **curiosity** ("your termination paperwork") to bypass scrutiny.
3. **The payload.** A link to a credential-harvesting page, a malicious attachment (macro-enabled Office doc, PDF with a link, ISO/zip hiding an executable), or instructions to read back an MFA code.
4. **Exploitation.** Once the attacker has access (password, session token, or code execution), they move laterally, establish persistence, and exfiltrate data — often within *minutes*.

The most effective lures share common traits: they look like normal business email, reference real people or events (spear phishing), arrive during busy periods, and ask the recipient to do something they *routinely* do — log into a portal, open a shared file, approve a payment.
