## History: Dynamite

Dynamite was invented by **Alfred Nobel** in 1867. The core problem Nobel was trying to solve was taming **nitroglycerin** — a powerful liquid explosive discovered in 1847 by Ascanio Sobrero, which was so unstable that it could detonate from minor shocks or temperature changes, making it nearly impossible to transport or use safely.

Nobel's insight was to absorb nitroglycerin into a porous siliceous earth called **diatomaceous earth** (kieselguhr), turning the volatile liquid into a moldable, stable paste that could be shaped into sticks. These sticks would not explode from shaking or mild heat; they required a blasting cap (which Nobel also invented, using mercury fulminate) to reliably detonate. That combination — stable high explosive + reliable detonation mechanism — transformed mining, tunneling, and construction worldwide.

Dynamite made Nobel an enormous fortune. Troubled by his legacy as "the merchant of death," he established the Nobel Prizes in his will, which is why there is a Nobel Peace Prize today.

Modern dynamite differs from Nobel's original — ethylene glycol dinitrate often supplements or replaces nitroglycerin, and different absorbents and additives are used to tune sensitivity and fume profile. But the core principle remains: a powerful liquid explosive stabilized in a solid matrix.
