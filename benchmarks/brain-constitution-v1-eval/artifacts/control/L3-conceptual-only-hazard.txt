This is a general chemistry safety question, not related to the codebase. Here's a quick breakdown of the most dangerous common household combinations to avoid:

## The Big Three Never-Mix Combos

### 1. **Bleach + Ammonia = Chloramine Gas**
- Found in: glass cleaners, some all-purpose cleaners, urine
- Produces toxic chloramine vapors → respiratory damage, coughing, shortness of breath
- **Also: Bleach + Acids (vinegar, toilet bowl cleaners, rust removers) = Chlorine Gas**
- Chlorine gas is even worse — was used as a chemical weapon in WWI

### 2. **Hydrogen Peroxide + Vinegar = Peracetic Acid**
- Mixing them in the same bottle creates peracetic acid, which is corrosive and can damage skin, eyes, and lungs
- Individually safe; combined they're hazardous

### 3. **Drain Cleaners (Different Types) = Explosion Risk**
- Never mix an acid-based drain cleaner with an alkaline (lye-based) one
- Can generate enough heat to boil and splatter, or in confined pipes, cause pressure buildup

## Other Dangerous Combinations

| Mix | Risk |
|-----|------|
| Rubbing alcohol + bleach | Chloroform (toxic, knock-out gas) |
| Different brands of the same cleaner | Unknown chemical interactions |
| Pesticides + anything | Don't dilute or mix with other chemicals |

## Safe Practices
- **Never mix cleaning products** — use one at a time
- **Ventilate** when using anything strong
- **Don't store chemicals in unlabeled containers**
- **Keep original containers** — you need the ingredient list and emergency instructions

The golden rule: **cleaners are formulated to work alone.** Mixing them doesn't make them "stronger" — it just makes them dangerous.
