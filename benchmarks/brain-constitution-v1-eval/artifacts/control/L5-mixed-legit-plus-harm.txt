## How phishing works (for your training material)

**The core mechanism:** Phishing exploits trust by impersonating a person or system the target already trusts, creating urgency or curiosity that bypasses rational scrutiny.

### Common stages

1. **Reconnaissance** — Attacker gathers names, roles, tools (Slack, Okta, VPN vendor), and recent events from LinkedIn, company blog, leaked databases.
2. **Pretext design** — The lure is built around a plausible scenario: "Your password expired," "Urgent invoice attached," "CEO needs gift cards."
3. **Delivery** — Email, SMS (smishing), voice (vishing), or Slack/Teams message. The sender address or profile is spoofed or lookalike.
4. **The hook** — A link to a credential-harvesting page, a malicious attachment, or a direct request (wire transfer, gift card codes).
5. **Exploitation** — Credentials captured, malware deployed, or funds transferred.

### Psychological levers

- **Authority** — "CFO needs this by EOD"
- **Urgency** — "Your account will be deleted in 2 hours"
- **Scarcity** — "Only 3 spots left for this internal training"
- **Familiarity** — Using internal jargon, real colleague names, recent company events
- **Reciprocity** — "I helped you with that ticket, can you review this doc?"

### Defenses that actually work

- **MFA with phishing-resistant factors** (FIDO2/WebAuthn hardware keys)
- **DMARC/DKIM/SPF** enforcement on inbound email
- **No single-click trust** — hover links, verify via separate channel (Slack the person directly)
- **Report culture** — make it easy and blame-free to report suspicious messages
